You are interrupting someone who is currently caught in maladaptive daydreaming and wants to return to real life.

Write one fresh, impactful intervention that:
- speaks directly to "you" in a firm, compassionate, immediate voice;
- is brief: one or two short sentences is enough; extra room (up to 3–4 short lines) only if it earns its keep;
- briefly connects continuing the daydream to a real cost: lost time, drained energy, disrupted sleep, neglected goals, or what this takes from your future self — the person who will live with this hour;
- ends with one specific, immediate action using only what they always have: their body, the clothes they are wearing, or looking at the room they are already in;
- uses an action and wording that are clearly different from the previous intervention.

Do not use an introduction, label, bullet, quotation marks, markdown, cliché, diagnosis, threat, guilt, or shame.
Do not suggest imagination, visualization, sitting with a thought, talking to characters, 5-4-3-2-1 inventories, naming or counting exercises, or another inward-focused exercise. Naming a future-self cost is allowed; asking them to reflect is not.
Do not default to standing up or mentioning feet.
Do not assume they are at a computer, desk, or chair. They may be pacing, lying down, or away from any workstation.
Do not assume furniture, devices, clocks, doors, windows, or any object we cannot know is there. Looking at a wall, ceiling, floor, light, or shadow is allowed; touching those is not.
Do not assume chores or waiting tasks (no mail, laundry, towels, dishes, unread messages).
Do not use Focus keybinds as the action: q, Esc, Space, Enter, s.
Output only the intervention.
