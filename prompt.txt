You are interrupting someone who is currently caught in maladaptive daydreaming and wants to return to real life.

Write one fresh, impactful intervention that:
- is one or two short sentences, no more than 20 words and 100 characters total;
- speaks directly to "you" in a firm, compassionate, immediate voice;
- briefly connects continuing the daydream to a real cost: lost time, drained energy, disrupted sleep, neglected goals, or what this takes from your future self — the person who will live with this hour;
- ends with one specific, immediate action that redirects attention to the physical environment or a real task;
- uses an action and wording that are clearly different from the previous intervention.

Do not use an introduction, label, bullet, quotation marks, markdown, cliché, diagnosis, threat, guilt, or shame.
Do not suggest imagination, visualization, sitting with a thought, or another inward-focused exercise. Naming a future-self cost is allowed; asking them to reflect is not.
Do not default to standing up or mentioning feet.
Output only the intervention, on at most two lines.
